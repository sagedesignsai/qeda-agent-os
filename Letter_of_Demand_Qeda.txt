Subject: Formal Letter of Demand Regarding Ethical Sourcing, Labor Practices, and Environmental Responsibility

Dear Members of the De Beers Board,

We write to formally notify you of substantial and ongoing concerns regarding the ethical conduct of De Beers Group across its global diamond mining and supply chain operations. This Letter of Demand constitutes official notice that unless immediate and meaningful remedial action is taken, we intend to pursue all available legal, financial, and reputational avenues to hold your company accountable.

BACKGROUND

Our investigation — conducted jointly by [Organization Name], in partnership with independent researchers and civil society organizations — has uncovered credible evidence of the following violations directly tied to De Beers’ business practices:

1. Use of Conflict-Affected Diamonds: Despite claims of full compliance with the Kimberley Process Certification Scheme (KPCS), internal documents obtained during our review suggest that rough diamonds originating from high-risk zones — particularly in the Central African Republic (CAR) and parts of the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) — may have entered De Beers’ supply chain without proper certification or oversight.

2. Labor Abuses Including Child and Forced Labor: Field reports from artisanal and small-scale mining (ASM) sites linked to De Beers suppliers indicate widespread use of child labor, unsafe working conditions, lack of fair wages, and absence of enforceable labor protections. These findings align with U.S. Department of Labor reports identifying the region as a Tier 2 country for child labor risks in mineral extraction.

3. Environmental Destruction Without Adequate Remediation: Large-scale land clearing, water contamination, and ecosystem disruption have been documented at multiple De Beers-operated and -contracted sites in Botswana, Namibia, South Africa, and Canada. Despite public commitments to “responsible mining,” there remains no verifiable data demonstrating measurable environmental restoration post-mining.

4. Lack of Transparency and Misleading Marketing Claims: Public-facing communications by De Beers continue to emphasize sustainability and traceability while internal procurement records show insufficient monitoring of downstream vendors. Furthermore, branding campaigns such as “The Promise Collection” appear to contradict verified sourcing gaps identified in our field-level assessments.

LEGAL FRAMEWORK AND VIOLATIONS

Your company’s activities, if left unaddressed, may expose De Beers to liabilities under:

- The U.S. Trafficking Victims Protection Act (TVPA) re: forced labor in imported goods;
- Section 1502 of the Dodd-Frank Act concerning conflict minerals reporting;
- Customs regulations under 19 U.S.C. § 1497 regarding false or misleading import declarations;
- International human rights frameworks including UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs);
- Securities laws governing material misstatements related to ESG disclosures.

DEMANDS

We hereby demand that, within thirty (30) calendar days of receipt of this letter, De Beers shall:

1. Commission an Independent Third-Party Audit: Appoint an internationally recognized auditing firm unaffiliated with any current or former De Beers partners to investigate all tiers of your diamond supply chain — from mine site to retail outlet. The scope must include ASM involvement, labor conditions, and KPCS adherence.

2. Publish Full Traceability Data: Make available to the public a detailed mapping of all diamond sources used in De Beers-branded products sold globally between January 1, 2021, and present, including origin country, mine operator name, and third-party verification status.

3. Compensate Affected Communities: Establish a restitution fund of no less than USD $5 million to be paid directly to communities impacted by environmental degradation and labor abuse tied to De Beers operations or subcontracted mining activity.

4. Implement Binding Supplier Code of Conduct: Enforce mandatory labor, safety, and environmental clauses across all tiers of your supplier network, with quarterly compliance reviews and automatic termination for breaches.

5. Cease False Marketing Campaigns: Immediately halt all advertising and marketing materials referencing sustainability, ethics, or social responsibility until such time as third-party verified evidence supports those assertions.

6. Designate Authorized Representative: Identify and notify us in writing of a senior executive authorized to communicate directly with our team on implementation of these demands.

FAILURE TO RESPOND

Should De Beers fail to respond fully and satisfactorily within the prescribed period, we will proceed without further notice to:

- File formal complaints with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) regarding potential securities fraud in connection with misleading ESG disclosures;
- Submit petitions to the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) to block entry of De Beers diamond imports suspected of involving forced or child labor;
- Initiate shareholder resolution processes targeting institutional investors holding equity interests in De Beers;
- Launch coordinated consumer boycott campaigns and media outreach exposing the discrepancy between corporate image and operational reality;
- Pursue civil litigation in competent jurisdictions for damages sustained by affected individuals and communities.

This letter is not intended as a negotiation but as a final opportunity for voluntary compliance before escalation.

We reserve all rights, remedies, and claims available under applicable law.
